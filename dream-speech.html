Dream Speech

The Global Women's Healing Leadership Award – December 10, 2051

Members of the Global Women's Healing Leadership Award committee, thank you for this honor. To be chosen for the women we've trained, the families we've fed, and the circles where women came to heal — that is something I'll carry for the rest of my life.

When I found out about this award, I thought about the little girl I used to be. There were many times my mom couldn't put food on the table. Abuse. Trauma. Neglect. But I'll never forget how my journey began.

My first job was at McDonald's, my junior year of high school. When the bell rang, I rode several buses over an hour to Blue Island for seven dollars an hour. Nobody taught me how to budget, so I taught myself. I split every check in half, and I still manage my money that way today. Watching coworkers stuck there for years, I realized money alone wasn't what I valued. I wanted my talents to matter, and I wanted money to support that instead of control me.

Later, I got into door-to-door sales, selling electricity and natural gas. I went a whole month without a single sale. I still knocked on every door, but I didn't believe in what I was selling, and I didn't believe in myself. My leaders had a saying: never go home with a donut you can't eat. A donut meant a zero. I went home with plenty of them. Then one of my leaders taught me how to carry myself at the door. I stopped letting the fear of rejection win. And I did. I went on to lead a team of seven to the top ten in the country.

Leading taught me I'm direct, and that I can break anything down step by step, usually with a good movie analogy. Most of all, there's nothing I can't do when I put my mind to it. That's the same belief that built the House of Nova.

House of Nova started in my mother's home during COVID in 2021: waist beads, then fifteen-dollar bracelets. In 2026, I joined Sunshine Enterprise Community Business Academy. It taught me my worth, how to price it, and how to structure the business I wanted to build.

But I still needed to fund it. So that's how I stumbled upon i.c.stars. It taught me the mindset and strategy of a true leader. After i.c.stars, I spent about a year as a business and data analyst, but I kept being drawn to the bigger picture. So I spent three years in project management. It felt like home. It was a juggle: I packed Nova orders before work and answered customers on my lunch break.

Then I found a nonprofit serving youth who had faced domestic violence and homelessness. I found it through Lyte Collective, the program that helped my sister and me find housing when we needed it most, thanks to my social worker, Julie. Julie, when I didn't know which direction I was going, you gave me certainty, and the reassurance to believe in myself. Whenever I needed something, or someone to advocate for me, it was you.

At that nonprofit, I went from program manager to three years as program director, then five years as executive director. The hardest part was letting people go when they weren't meeting our standard, but I learned to protect the mission. My proudest moment? The little girl whose mom couldn't always put food on the table made sure thousands of families had something to eat.

Then I spent four years as Chief Operating Officer of a wellness company, opening wellness centers in overlooked neighborhoods. I learned to run operations at scale, and I poured all of it into Nova. When I left, it was because Nova needed all of me.

Today, House of Nova is a multi-million-dollar brand: storefronts, spiritual jewelry, a book club, and Healing Circles on six continents. But it was never just about jewelry. It was about helping women see themselves as confident, strong, and beautiful. And to my day-one girlies in your Nova Bae gear: I see you.

None of it was easy. I faced systemic oppression young, and self-doubt followed me long after i.c.stars. i.c.stars taught me to let everyone feel heard without letting their opinions define me. In one assignment, my word was disrupting. Sometimes something needs to stop so we can see the truth. I didn't just become a disruptor. I am one.

Mia, my little sister, you remind me who I am. I wanted to show you that where we start doesn't decide where we finish. Saturn, my niece, you are the beat of my heart. Helping raise you let me break the cycle your mom and I grew up with.

Brandies, on my first day, you didn't know me from a can of paint. I told you I didn't have any food to eat, and you made sure I ate. I'll never forget that. You and the whole i.c.stars staff held us to a standard before we believed we could reach it. Thank you for seeing more in me than I saw in myself. And to my cohort and our mentors, thank you for pushing me to grow.

What's next is the Woven Women Foundation: training and healing centers for women in overlooked communities. My goal is one million women with a path to meaningful work and a place to heal.

So here's my challenge to you. Look at the person in your life who's been underestimated. Maybe it's the person in the mirror. Say out loud what she could become, and then help her build it.

A friend told me about i.c.stars when I was just looking for another IT program. I didn't know it would change my life. The girl who rode buses to Blue Island for seven dollars an hour now helps women build the lives they deserve. This is my full-circle moment. Thank you.
