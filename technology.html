Debugging: The Floating Tiles

While scrolling down my portfolio, I noticed my Level Select tiles were floating on top of the header instead of sliding under it. I learned the cause was the transform I used to make the tiles lift on hover, which puts them on their own layer above the header. The fix was one line of CSS: adding z-index: 10 to the header so it always stays on top. It taught me that every style choice can have side effects, so I need to be specific about how I want each part of my page to behave.

Where It Clicked: Task 5

Coding clicked for me during Geek Week, around Task 5, when we moved into Excel. I had used Excel at a technology high school, and I realized the formulas followed the same patterns I learned in math. It felt like receiving downloads: my brain was doing a wide search of everything I had ever learned to make all this new information digestible in a short time. I also learned how I react under stress and how to push past it while staying focused. Going forward, I know I won't always understand everything at first, but if I push and encourage myself in a healthy way, I will succeed.

To Disrupt: Choose Your Path

To Disrupt is an interactive walkthrough game: a web app that turns my Planetary Responsibility into something you play instead of just read. Each of my 5 gifts is a chapter where you meet the gift, choose how to use it, train through real-life quests, unlock the jobs it leads to, and beat a boss quiz to move on. I designed the game, wrote the gift names and quiz questions, and used my own illustrations; AI tools helped me build the React code and animations and fix bugs. Some animations and formatting broke along the way, which taught me that fixing or adding one thing can break another, so I play through every change before I ship it. It taught me to break a big idea into small pieces and ship them one at a time, which is how I run projects.

GitHub: Checkpoints, Branches, and Shipping

GitHub changed how I protect and share my work. Every change I make becomes a commit: a saved checkpoint with a short message explaining what changed, so I can trace my history or undo a mistake with git restore. I built this portfolio on a separate branch, build-portfolio, so I could experiment freely without risking the live site, then brought the finished work into main. GitHub also hosts my work: this portfolio and three other projects are live on GitHub Pages, and every push updates them within minutes.

Business Tools: Running a Project with Plaky, Teams, and Outlook

As Project Manager and Scrum Lead for Canary Consultants, I relied on three tools to keep a five-person team on track. In Plaky, I built and managed three sprint backlogs, including a 34-item product backlog and a 92-story-point sprint, and re-prioritized tasks whenever our timeline shifted. Microsoft Teams was where we met, shared files, and kept our daily communication in one place, while Outlook kept meetings with Medline stakeholders and i.c.stars mentors scheduled and documented. Together, these tools turned a fast-moving client project into a plan the whole team could see and follow.
