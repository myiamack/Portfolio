Career: Where it all comes together

Target Role: Project Manager

I am pursuing a career in project management because it aligns with my purpose. My strength is seeing what's missing and what's not being said that's needed, then bridging the gaps between the client and the team. I keep the product at the center of every conversation so the vision the client brings to us becomes the solution they receive. Leading Canary Consultants on our Medline project confirmed that this is the work I'm built for.

Where I'm Headed: 1 Year and 5 Years

Within the next year, my goal is to step into a Project Manager role, leading teams from kickoff to delivery and building a track record of projects that meet their scope, timeline, and client expectations. Within five years, I plan to grow into a Program Manager, overseeing multiple related projects and aligning them to a larger business strategy. Each step builds on the same strength: seeing the bigger picture and turning it into a plan people can follow. Alongside my career, I'll continue growing House of Nova into an established LLC, using the same planning and client skills I bring to every project.

My Brand: Adaptability, Integrity, and Love

Three values guide how I work. Adaptability shows up when plans change: when our Medline timeline shifted, I re-prioritized our backlog and adjusted scope so the team could still deliver. Integrity means staying true to myself even when no one is watching, and doing the right thing without needing validation for it. Love shows up as support and care for others without expecting anything in return, like writing a Geek Week study guide for classmates who needed it. "I am not here to make a living; I am here to make a difference." That is the mantra I live by.

Resume & Job Prep: From Generic to Targeted

Feedback transformed my resume in four ways. First, I added metrics so my impact is measurable, like coaching a sales team to a 67% increase in daily sales and managing a 34-item product backlog. Second, I rewrote my bullet points using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result), so each one tells a complete story. Third, I stopped using one generic resume and now tailor it to the role I'm pursuing, starting with project management. Finally, I reorganized my sections so the most relevant experience and skills appear first, with supporting details placed where they still add value.
