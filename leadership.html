Leadership: growing by lifting others

Leadership in Service: The Geek Week Survival Guide

Nobody asked me to write a study guide during Geek Week. I wrote it because I saw classmates struggling without support, and I had just worked through the same mistakes myself. I turned every SQL and Excel error I made into a lesson with the fix and an analogy, and shaped it so it could be passed down to the next cohort. Leading this way taught me that lifting others is also how I learn: if I can't explain something in my own words, I don't fully understand it yet.

Buffalo Soldiers: Leading Myself First

The Buffalo Soldiers workshop asked me to build an army out of my own strengths. I chose to face a real challenge: being interrupted while sharing ideas in team meetings. Mapping eight soldiers, four personal and four professional, showed me which strengths I lean on, where each one falls short, and how they cover for one another. Leading others starts with leading myself, and this project gave me a plan to make sure my voice is heard so I can make room for other voices too.

Year of the Horse: Leading Toward a Vision

The Year of the Horse workshop asked us to declare the future we are willing to grow into. I built mine as a museum-wall timeline that starts in 2054 and walks backward to 2025, so each milestone shows what it took to get there. Working backward from a long-range vision taught me to set goals the way a project manager plans a launch: start with the outcome, then map the steps that lead to it.

Team Week: Stepping Into Project Manager

During Team Week, I naturally stepped into the project manager role. I see the overall vision first, then break it into smaller, tangible action plans the team can complete one at a time to reach the bigger goal. Just as important, I learned to translate that plan for each teammate and meet people where they are, so everyone understands their part and why it matters. That week showed me that organizing people around a shared goal is something that comes naturally to me, and it is the strength I now bring to every project.

Feedback: Letting Go of How I'm Perceived

One of the most valuable pieces of feedback I received was to stop letting worry about how others perceive me hold me back. That concern used to make me second-guess myself before speaking up. Since then, I focus on whether my ideas help the team rather than on how I come across, and I share them with more confidence. Letting go of that worry has made me a clearer communicator and a steadier leader.

Gifts Project: My Planetary Responsibility, To Disrupt

My Gifts Project gave me one word for my purpose: Disrupt, meaning seeing what's broken, saying what others won't, and standing with people until it changes for the better. I identified five gifts that carry that purpose (Expose, Challenge, Advocate, Empower, and Support), mapped each one to two career paths, and committed to five concrete actions per gift to keep growing them. I then turned this work into an interactive game, so anyone can walk through my gifts and see how they connect to the leader I am becoming.

Dream Speech: My Full-Circle Moment

My Dream Speech imagines me in 2051, accepting the Global Women's Healing Leadership Award, and it shows me as both a business leader and a service leader. It starts with the little girl whose mom couldn't always put food on the table, and the first job I rode buses over an hour to reach. It traces the path I am committing to: leading a sales team of seven into the national top ten, i.c.stars, project and program management, leading a nonprofit for youth facing domestic violence and homelessness, and serving as Chief Operating Officer of a wellness company. It honors the people who carried me, from my social worker at Lyte Collective to Brandies and the i.c.stars staff, and it ends with House of Nova grown into a global brand and my next mission, the Woven Women Foundation, built to give one million women a path to meaningful work and a place to heal. Where I start doesn't decide where I finish.

Tea Host: Welcoming Jennifer and Jill

I served as Tea Host for two guests, Jennifer and Jill. To prepare, I researched each guest on LinkedIn, looking for experiences I could relate to and key points worth expanding on, so our conversation could go deeper than introductions. Hosting taught me that thoughtful preparation is a form of respect: when you show up knowing someone's story, they open up and share more of it.
